A Devotion

Looking at Him, Loved Him

Mark 10:1–31 · three scenes on the way into Judea · WEB

1 He arose from there and came into the borders of Judea and beyond the Jordan. Multitudes came together to him again. As he usually did, he was again teaching them. 2 Pharisees came to him testing him, and asked him, “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife?” 3 He answered, “What did Moses command you?” 4 They said, “Moses allowed a certificate of divorce to be written, and to divorce her.” 5 But Jesus said to them, “For your hardness of heart, he wrote you this commandment. 6 But from the beginning of the creation, God made them male and female. 7 For this cause a man will leave his father and mother, and will join to his wife, 8 and the two will become one flesh, so that they are no longer two, but one flesh. 9 What therefore God has joined together, let no man separate.” 10 In the house, his disciples asked him again about the same matter. 11 He said to them, “Whoever divorces his wife, and marries another, commits adultery against her. 12 If a woman herself divorces her husband, and marries another, she commits adultery.” 13 They were bringing to him little children, that he should touch them, but the disciples rebuked those who were bringing them. 14 But when Jesus saw it, he was moved with indignation, and said to them, “Allow the little children to come to me! Don’t forbid them, for God’s Kingdom belongs to such as these. 15 Most certainly I tell you, whoever will not receive God’s Kingdom like a little child, he will in no way enter into it.” 16 He took them in his arms, and blessed them, laying his hands on them. 17 As he was going out into the way, one ran to him, knelt before him, and asked him, “Good Teacher, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?” 18 Jesus said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except one—God. 19 You know the commandments: ‘Do not murder,’ ‘Do not commit adultery,’ ‘Do not steal,’ ‘Do not give false testimony,’ ‘Do not defraud,’ ‘Honor your father and mother.’ ” 20 He said to him, “Teacher, I have observed all these things from my youth.” 21 Jesus looking at him loved him, and said to him, “One thing you lack. Go, sell whatever you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me, taking up the cross.” 22 But his face fell at that saying, and he went away sorrowful, for he was one who had great possessions. 23 Jesus looked around, and said to his disciples, “How difficult it is for those who have riches to enter into God’s Kingdom!” 24 The disciples were amazed at his words. But Jesus answered again, “Children, how hard it is for those who trust in riches to enter into God’s Kingdom! 25 It is easier for a camel to go through a needle’s eye than for a rich man to enter into God’s Kingdom.” 26 They were exceedingly astonished, saying to him, “Then who can be saved?” 27 Jesus, looking at them, said, “With men it is impossible, but not with God, for all things are possible with God.” 28 Peter began to tell him, “Behold, we have left all, and have followed you.” 29 Jesus said, “Most certainly I tell you, there is no one who has left house, or brothers, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or land, for my sake, and for the sake of the Good News, 30 but he will receive one hundred times more now in this time: houses, brothers, sisters, mothers, children, and land, with persecutions; and in the age to come eternal life. 31 But many who are first will be last; and the last first.”

The text

Three scenes. Pharisees ask about divorce “in order to test him”, and Jesus goes behind what Moses wrote for hard hearts to “the beginning of creation”. The disciples turn children away, and Jesus is indignant: the kingdom belongs to such, and is entered only by one who receives it “like a child”. Then a man runs up, kneels, and asks what he must do to inherit eternal life. Mark sets receive and what must I do side by side and does not comment.

The man has kept the commandments from his youth, and Jesus does not dispute it. Mark says that Jesus, looking at him, loved him, and then spoke: one thing lacking, sell all, give to the poor, “and come, follow me.” He goes away sorrowful. Mark does not say what became of him. The disciples ask who, then, can be saved, and the answer names no method: “With man it is impossible, but not with God.” Peter's “we have left everything” is met with a hundredfold, “with persecutions”.

Tim Keller

“Greed: The Case of the Rich Young Ruler”, a sermon on Matthew 19:16–25, the parallel account, preached in 1995. Read from a machine-made transcript. It covers only the rich man.

Keller asks why the man went away grieved, and gives four reasons. First, he met the real Jesus, whose message always shocks in two directions:

It demands more than you thought and it offers more than you thought.

Second, the man came with two assumptions, that “Christianity is something you can add and you can do.” Jesus breaks both. Third, Jesus got personal, and here Keller leaves Matthew for Mark's detail: he looked at him and loved him. Keller reads the command as surgery on one man, not a law for all. In this sermon he says there is no rule that every Christian must give up everything; Jesus is after one thing, and Keller gives him the words: “I want the thing that you think will give you a life of power and joy without God.”

Fourth, what the man lacks is not the giving away. It is the treasure in heaven. Keller ends by turning the phrase round, in God's voice: “if you make my son your treasure, that makes you my treasure.”

The tradition · Athanasius of Alexandria

Life of St. Anthony, chapters 1–3. A saint's life, not a commentary; Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers translation. The sentence Antony hears is Matthew's, “If you would be perfect”, not Mark's “You lack one thing”.

Athanasius tells of a young Egyptian of a wealthy Christian family, eighteen or twenty, his parents not six months dead, a little sister in his care. Walking to church he is thinking of how the Apostles left all.

Pondering over these things he entered the church, and it happened the Gospel was being read, and he heard the Lord saying to the rich man … Antony, as though God had put him in mind of the Saints, and the passage had been read on his account, went out immediately from the church, and gave the possessions of his forefathers to the villagers

He kept a little back for his sister. Then, in church again, he heard “be not anxious for the morrow”, and gave that away as well. What Athanasius marks in him is attention: “he had given such heed to what was read that none of the things that were written fell from him to the ground”.

The two voices part here. Keller says the command was for one man and is no rule. Athanasius sets down Antony's keeping of it to the letter as the beginning of a holy life.

Paul

The disciples ask who can be saved if a rich man cannot let go. Paul, writing about a gift for the relief of the saints, names one who was rich and became poor, and calls the giving of the Macedonians grace.

1 Moreover, brothers, we make known to you the grace of God which has been given in the assemblies of Macedonia, 2 how in much proof of affliction, the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded to the riches of their generosity. 3 For according to their power, I testify, yes and beyond their power, they gave of their own accord, 4 begging us with much entreaty to receive this grace and the fellowship in the service to the saints. 5 This was not as we had expected, but first they gave their own selves to the Lord, and to us through the will of God. 6 So we urged Titus, that as he had made a beginning before, so he would also complete in you this grace. 7 But as you abound in everything, in faith, utterance, knowledge, all earnestness, and in your love to us, see that you also abound in this grace. 8 I speak not by way of commandment, but as proving through the earnestness of others the sincerity also of your love. 9 For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that you through his poverty might become rich.

Which sentence of this passage, if any, sounds as though it had been read on your account?

Which is harder to believe: that he asks for everything, or that he looked at the man and loved him?
